Finance Is Deliberately Confusing. These 3 Numbers Are Literally All That Matters.

Young Investor Team · April 15, 2025 · 5 min read

The financial industry profits from your confusion.
The more overwhelmed you feel, the more advice you pay for.
The more jargon they use, the more dependent you become.
Here is what they do not want you to know: 90% of your long-term wealth comes from three numbers.
Everything else is noise.

Number 1: Monthly contribution

Not your lump sum. Not your total savings. Not your salary. Your monthly contribution — the fixed amount that moves into investments automatically, every month, regardless of what markets are doing.

This is the most powerful number in personal finance. More important than your stock picks. More important than timing. More important than your annual return rate.

Consistency beats brilliance

A person who invests $200/month for 30 years into a basic index fund will almost always outperform someone who invests $5,000 once and waits. Frequency compounds. Single decisions do not.

Set this number to something you will not miss. Not impressive. Consistent.

Number 2: Return rate

The annual percentage your money grows. For most beginners, the goal is simple: match the market.

The S&P 500 has returned approximately 10% per year on average over the past century. An index ETF like VOO or SPY matches that return automatically, at almost zero cost.

Index fund (market return)
~10%/yr
Average active fund after fees
~7-8%/yr

Professional stock pickers underperform the index 80-90% of the time over 20 years. Documented data. Decades of research. You do not need to beat the market. You need to be the market.

Number 3: Time

The variable no financial advisor can sell you. The one you cannot buy back. The one that matters more than your salary, your picks, and the entire economy combined.

The annual cost of waiting

At 22, every year you delay costs approximately $14,000-$25,000 in final wealth. That is not potential. That is the compounded arithmetic of inaction — calculated, predictable, and permanent.

Time is the only number on this list you cannot improve after the fact. The other two can be adjusted later. Time cannot.

Everything else can wait

Options trading, crypto, individual stocks, technical analysis, sector rotation — these are all real things. They also have nothing to do with building wealth as a beginner. Get the three numbers locked in first. Automate them. Then learn the rest from a position of actual security.
